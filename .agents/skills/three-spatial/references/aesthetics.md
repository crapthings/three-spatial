# Aesthetic judgment and refinement

Use for scene creation, visual polish, or a requested aesthetic review. Judge the work against its intended experience and [creative direction](creative-direction.md), not a universal style. An existing brief may already supply that direction; no new brainstorming is necessary.

## Assess relationships, not decoration count

Consider the relevant criteria together:

- **Hierarchy:** what attracts attention first, what supports it, and what can recede? Multiple focal points can be intentional if their relationship is clear.
- **Unity and variation:** do forms, materials, and motifs belong together while retaining useful differences? Coherence need not mean uniformity.
- **Rhythm and contrast:** do repetition, grouping, scale changes, and interruptions create the intended pace? Variation need not mean independent random noise.
- **Mass and negative space:** do occupied and quiet regions support the intended balance, tension, or density? Neither symmetry nor spaciousness is a default goal.
- **Economy and character:** which decisions make the work recognizable, and which details merely compete for attention? Complexity is useful only when it serves the experience.

Review the whole, the affected assemblies, and the whole again. Local attractiveness does not guarantee a strong composition. Consider the intended camera or range of views; a deliberate single-view composition and an explorable scene have different needs.

## Refine at the right level

Identify the weak relationship and choose the smallest meaningful intervention: remove or regroup detail, alter emphasis, redistribute mass, adjust a silhouette, or strengthen a motif. If the concept itself is unclear, return to creative direction rather than polishing every component. Do not equate polish with more geometry, more colors, or more realism.

Use [proportions.md](proportions.md) for relative dimensions, [layout.md](layout.md) for composition and spacing, and [color.md](color.md) for palette/material/light decisions. Recheck functional clearances, support, and surface integrity after changes. Preserve intentional awkwardness, density, or dissonance when it serves the brief.

## Ground the judgment

Distinguish an intended effect, an observed issue, and a proposed improvement. Name the relationship and its consequence rather than declaring something “ugly” or assigning an invented beauty score. Compare permitted before/after evidence when available. With code-only access, evaluate authored relationships and likely consequences while leaving rendered appearance unverified. Iterate to resolve identified weaknesses, not to generate endless alternatives after the work meets the brief.
